DIRTY LAUNDRY: MURDER IN THE CAYMANS
Four-Round Plot Summaries
Two Selected Endings: Gloria Reyes (Path 5) and Richie Suicide (Path 10)

======================================================================
PATH 5: GLORIA REYES IS THE KILLER
======================================================================

ROUND 1 — "THE AFTER-PARTY" (45 minutes)

It's Friday night at Heathens Hollow, a boutique hotel in the Grand Caymans. A "Tax Optimization Seminar" — really a networking event for money launderers and white-collar criminals — just wrapped its closing dinner. The after-party is underway in the hotel courtyard. Tiki torches, steel drums, a glowing pool.

The guests mingle. Val Montecito, the hotel owner, holds court with champagne. Dex Calloway works the bar, making drinks and reading the room. Marco Reyes, the valet, pops in and out. Zane Nakamura talks crypto on his phone. Gloria Reyes arrives in linen, asking about everyone's families, speaking so softly people lean in. Pam Worthington is already drunk, ordering stronger drinks and joking about finding husband number two. Irina Volkov, head of housekeeping, works quietly in the background. Sunny Patel blends in, forgettable, talking corporate buzzwords.

Doug Kaminski is conspicuously absent — reportedly stuck in Pittsburgh with a porta potty emergency.

The conference organizer, Richard "Richie" Rathbone, was supposed to give a toast at 9 PM. He doesn't show. Val calls his room. No answer. Dex confirms Richie ordered vodka sent to his room at 7 PM.

At some point after 10 PM, a scream from the service area. Irina comes running out, panicked. She found Richie face-down in a laundry basket in the laundry room. Dead. Blue lips. Chemical residue on his shirt collar. An empty vodka bottle on the floor. His phone is cracked, battery dead.

Rosa Martinez, the night manager, locks down the hotel. Police are two hours away due to an incident on the main island. Nobody leaves. The round ends with the realization: one of them did this.


ROUND 2 — "EVERYONE'S A SUSPECT" (60 minutes)

Everyone gathers in the lobby. Chip Davenport, a retired lawyer who's attended the conference for thirty years, suggests they investigate themselves. The alibi round begins.

Val says she was in her suite with Zane from 9:15 on. Zane confirms — too eagerly, too rehearsed. They were actually in crisis mode over Zane's compromised crypto wallet, but they won't say that. Marco claims he was at the valet stand all night, but his timestamps don't add up and he took a suspicious 15-minute break around 10 PM. Dex says he was behind the bar except for a 20-minute break — he won't say where he went. Gloria says she was in the conference room discussing tax strategies with Gerald Finch and Lorraine Chu, who confirm a little too readily. Pam can barely remember anything after 9:30 — she's fuzzy, crying, and has a chemical burn on her hand she can't explain. Doug wasn't even at the hotel yet, he claims. Irina says she was working laundry all night. Sunny lists every person she spoke to and when, so precisely it sounds rehearsed.

Secrets start surfacing. Photos emerge of Val and Richie arguing on the beach. Marco's rage over Richie calling him a "local punk" looks disproportionate. Pam and Richie were apparently having some kind of affair — a text from Richie says "we're done." The margarita that Dex spilled on Richie looks deliberate. Dex and Doug clearly know each other, which neither has mentioned.

Rosa drops the biggest bomb: a master key was used to access the service area at 10:07 PM. Only four people have master keys — Rosa (on a video call), Irina, Marco, and maintenance (off-island). There's also an owner's override card that belongs to Val. Marco says he used his key to move a car into the service garage. Rosa asks why he looks sick.

Accusations fly — Marco had motive and opportunity, Pam can't remember anything and has a chemical burn, Val and Zane's alibi is suspiciously airtight. The round ends with sirens approaching and the realization that everyone had a reason to want Richie dead.


ROUND 3 — EVIDENCE AND INVESTIGATION (75 minutes)

[Gloria path-specific beats]

The investigation deepens. Gloria's conference room alibi starts cracking. Gerald and Lorraine vouch for her, but under pressure, Gerald admits Gloria stepped out for "maybe five or ten minutes" around 10:25 PM. He didn't think it was important. She said she was going to the restroom.

The service stairwell has a side exit from the conference room. Three minutes from there to the service area. Gloria had a window.

Chip Davenport reveals that Gloria is Marco's mother. Not speculation — confirmed. "I've known Gloria for thirty years. Marco is her son." This reframes everything. Gloria and Marco's careful distance at the party wasn't professionalism — it was cover.

Diego's death surfaces. Chip continues: Gloria's firstborn son, Diego, was killed three years ago. Twenty-two years old. Richie provided intelligence to a rival cartel that led to a fatal ambush. Diego was collateral damage. Gloria has been carrying that for three years.

The poisoned shirt is traced back through hotel laundry. Irina delivered a garment bag to Richie's floor at 7 PM. Chemical analysis of the residue on Richie's collar matches a slow-acting dermal toxin — not something you find at a hotel. Someone supplied it. Under pressure, Irina breaks: Gloria gave her the chemical compound. Gloria ordered the shirt poisoned.

But the toxin was slow-acting. It would have weakened Richie over hours — headaches, dizziness, nausea — but wouldn't have killed him quickly. Something else finished the job. The autopsy reveals a puncture wound on Richie's neck consistent with an injection. A fast-acting paralytic agent. Not the same compound as the shirt.

Two separate attacks. The shirt was the slow setup. The injection was the kill shot.

Gloria's purse is searched. She carries an insulin pen. She's not diabetic. The pen contains traces of a paralytic agent purchased through cartel channels.

Sunny's cover is blown — she's revealed as a federal IRS/Interpol agent. Richie was her informant, planning to flip on everyone at the conference. This means Gloria wasn't just protecting herself from a personal grudge. Richie's testimony would have exposed her entire cartel operation. Diego's death was personal motive. The federal case was existential motive.

Marco, under pressure, admits he saw Gloria near the service stairwell around 10:25 PM. He didn't say anything because she's his mother. He's been protecting her all night.


ROUND 4 — CONFRONTATION AND CONFESSION (30 minutes)

Players vote on who they believe killed Richie. If they correctly identify Gloria, the confrontation plays out.

Gloria doesn't run. She doesn't deflect. She stands and looks at Marco.

She confirms everything. She ordered Irina to poison the shirt as a slow-acting setup. Then she left the conference room at 10:25 PM through the side exit, took the service stairwell, and found Richie in the service area near the laundry room. He was already deteriorating from the poisoned shirt — confused, stumbling, struggling to breathe.

He recognized her. "Gloria? What are you—"

She injected the paralytic through his neck. Ninety seconds. His nervous system shut down. He stopped breathing. She placed him in the laundry basket and was back in the conference room within fifteen minutes. Gerald and Lorraine didn't question her absence.

Her confession is about Diego. Her firstborn. Twenty-two. Killed because Richie gave one piece of intelligence to the wrong person and called it "cost of doing business." She waited three years. She organized the conference invitation. She positioned Irina. She brought the paralytic. Every step was deliberate.

She tells Marco she's sorry he had to see this. She tells the room that Richie killed her son and was about to destroy every person in the building with federal testimony. She doesn't ask for sympathy. She doesn't claim self-defense. She says: "A mother protects her children. Even the dead ones."

The game ends with Gloria being taken away. Marco is devastated. The room is forced to grapple with whether Gloria was justified — a grieving mother who executed the man responsible for her son's death, or a cartel operative who murdered a federal witness to protect her empire. The answer is both.


======================================================================
PATH 10: RICHIE RATHBONE KILLED HIMSELF (SUICIDE)
======================================================================

ROUND 1 — "THE AFTER-PARTY" (45 minutes)

Identical to Path 5. Same setting, same characters, same mingle, same discovery. Richie doesn't show for his toast. Irina finds him dead in the laundry basket. Chemical residue on his shirt. Empty vodka bottle. Rosa locks down the hotel. Everyone assumes murder.

The only difference the players won't notice: the scene is exactly what it looks like. There's no hidden killer. The laundry basket, the service area, the chemical residue — it's all Richie's doing.


ROUND 2 — "EVERYONE'S A SUSPECT" (60 minutes)

Identical to Path 5. Same alibis, same cracks, same revelations. Marco's suspicious break, Val and Zane's rehearsed alibi, Pam's blackout, Gloria's too-smooth story, Doug's absence. The master key swipe at 10:07 PM. All the same accusations.

The game functions normally. Players are investigating a murder that didn't happen. Every piece of evidence points somewhere — that's by design. Richie set it up so that everyone would look guilty.


ROUND 3 — EVIDENCE AND INVESTIGATION (75 minutes)

[Suicide path-specific beats]

Evidence keeps accumulating, but nothing resolves. Every trail leads to a different suspect. The investigation goes in circles.

The poisoned shirt is traced to Irina via Gloria's orders — same as every path. But the toxin was slow-acting. It would have taken hours to kill. And yet Richie also drank from a separately prepared vodka bottle. Chemical analysis reveals the vodka was spiked with a different, faster-acting compound — one Richie procured himself through encrypted channels. The shirt poison and the vodka poison are two different substances from two different sources.

Richie knew the shirt was poisoned. He'd arranged this kind of operation before — he recognized the symptoms. He chose not to remove the shirt. He supplemented it with his own spiked vodka. He accelerated what was already in motion.

His encrypted laptop is recovered. The files contain evidence on everyone — Val's trafficking operation, Gloria's cartel finances, Zane's skimming, Doug's laundering. But there's also a folder labeled "FINAL." Inside: a document addressed to no one in particular. Not a suicide note exactly — more of an accounting. A ledger of sins.

He details what he did to Diego — providing intelligence to a rival cartel that got a twenty-two-year-old killed. He calls it the moment he knew he was irredeemable. He details blackmailing Val for $500K while claiming to love her. He details betraying every client who trusted him. He details taking $50K in federal expense money and spending it on personal luxuries while pretending to cooperate with Sunny's investigation.

His phone records show he called Sunny at 9:40 PM and told her he couldn't testify. He said he still loved Val. He said he was disappearing. He hung up. Then he turned off his phone.

At 10:15 PM, he walked to the laundry room. The literal heart of "dirty laundry." He climbed into an empty basket. He lay down surrounded by other people's secrets, other people's soiled fabric. He died there at approximately 10:40 PM.

Sunny confirms the call. She's devastated — her three-year investigation collapsed because her informant chose to die rather than testify. She reveals she's a federal agent. She reveals Richie was going to bring down everyone in the room. And then he quit.

The evidence doesn't point to a killer because there is no killer. Every suspicious behavior — Marco's break, Val's crisis, Pam's blackout, Doug's late arrival, Gloria's stepped-out alibi — is real, but none of it is murder. Everyone was doing something shady that night. None of it was killing Richie.

Gloria ordered the poisoned shirt. That's attempted murder. But Richie knew about it and chose to wear it anyway. He used her attack as part of his own exit.


ROUND 4 — CONFRONTATION AND CONFESSION (30 minutes)

Players vote. They'll vote for someone — Marco, Gloria, Val, whoever they've built their case against. They'll be wrong.

No matter who they accuse, the accusation doesn't land. The accused denies it, and the evidence doesn't close. There's always a gap, always an alibi that holds just enough.

Then the host reveals the truth. There is no killer. Richie Rathbone committed suicide.

He staged his own death in a laundry basket as a final act of symbolism. Dirty laundry — literal and figurative. He poisoned himself with the spiked vodka, let the poisoned shirt accelerate the process, and walked himself to the place where secrets go to be cleaned.

The "FINAL" document is read aloud. Richie's accounting of his own sins. Diego. Val. The blackmail. The federal betrayal. Every person in the room is named. Every secret is exposed. His death is the delivery mechanism for the truth he was too cowardly to speak while alive.

He didn't kill himself to escape justice. He killed himself to deliver it. His suicide was arson — he burned everyone, including himself.

The game ends not with the satisfaction of catching a killer, but with a mirror. Every player spent four rounds building cases against each other, certain that someone in the room was a murderer. No one was. They were all guilty of other things — trafficking, cartel operations, money laundering, blackmail, obstruction — but not murder. The only person who killed anyone that night was the victim himself.

The room is left with uncomfortable questions. Is Gloria still guilty of attempted murder via the poisoned shirt? Is Irina complicit for following orders? Did Richie's suicide make him a victim or a final manipulator? There's no clean resolution. The genre promises a killer. This path refuses to deliver one.
