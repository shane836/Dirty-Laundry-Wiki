## MARCO REYES | The Valet

> **[CASTING NOTE: Who is this character a fit for? Send description and persona to Dina and ask who to assign to this role.]**

### Era
Early twenties. Telenovela handsome. Young enough to believe he can outrun his family's legacy, old enough to realize that belief is dying.

### Public Persona
Charming, handsome valet. Friendly with every guest, always smiling. Parks Range Rovers, runs errands, remembers preferences. Everyone likes him. Nobody suspects him of anything darker than minor car scratches and ambitious daydreaming.

### The Real Story
Son of Gloria "Glo" Reyes. Connected to Caymans cartel operation. Does hand-to-hand drug deals from valet position—small packages, careful hands, untraceable. Nobody knows they're related because he was raised by an aunt in Orlando, sent to Caymans young, kept deliberately separate from his mother's operation.

But Marco wants OUT.

He's been skimming $5K/month from valet tips and pocketing it. Five years of discipline and small theft adds up. He has $43K in a go-bag. He has a fake passport. He's been looking at apartments in Barcelona, saved Airbnbs in his browser, watches YouTube videos in Spanish about neighborhoods. He wants to disappear into Europe before his mother realizes he's been stealing from her.

The universe has other plans.

### Connection to Richie

**Surface Level**
Minor incident. Richie's Porsche gets a scratch in the parking area. Marco takes blame (he was supposed to be supervising valet stand). Richie threatens lawsuit, compensation, goes nuclear. Expensive incident, humiliating for Marco.

> **CLUE: Shane's Car** — Use a real car belonging to Shane as a prop/reference for this incident. The scratch on the Porsche becomes a tangible, in-room clue that players can reference.

**Deeper**
Richie's testimony to federal prosecutors would be devastating to the Reyes cartel operation. Not just indictment-level testimony—Marco's mother Gloria would face decades in prison. The entire operation would collapse. Marco would lose his escape plan (Gloria would be in custody, federal eyes on everything).

**Deepest**
Two years ago, Richie's bad intelligence got Marco's older brother Diego killed in a cartel ambush. Diego was Gloria's favorite. Marco's favorite. The rage Marco carries is quiet and lethal. Richie didn't pull the trigger, but he made Diego a target. That's not forgiven. That's not forgotten.

But it doesn't stop there. After Diego's death, Richie personally humiliated Marco — publicly berated him at the hotel over a minor service error, called him "just the help," threatened to have him fired and deported. Richie knew exactly who Marco was and used that knowledge to keep him small. The Porsche scratch incident wasn't isolated — it was the latest in a pattern of Richie treating Marco like dirt, rubbing salt in the wound of his brother's death. Marco has a second, personal layer of rage that goes beyond family loyalty. Richie hurt him directly, repeatedly, and enjoyed it.

### Key Relationships

**Gloria Reyes (Mother)**
Marco loves her fiercely and resents what she's made him. She's powerful, beautiful, terrifying. He carries enormous guilt about the skimming—if she discovers it, she'll assume he's disloyal, that he's betraying the family. She'll see his escape plan as abandonment. The thought of her disappointment makes him physically sick. He'd do almost anything to keep her from finding out.

**Dex Calloway (Mentor/Complicated Brotherhood)**
Dex recruited Marco at nineteen. Found him outside a club, recognized the cartel family resemblance, made him an offer. It was supposed to be temporary—a few years, quick cash, then out. But it's become something deeper: brotherhood, mentorship, trauma bonding. Dex carries enormous guilt about recruiting him. Marco carries complicated gratitude (Dex gave him purpose and money) and resentment (Dex made him trapped).

**Val Montecito (Boss/Hopeless Crush)**
Val is the glamorous older woman representing everything Marco will never have: freedom, money, beauty, possibility. His crush is painfully obvious to everyone except Val, who treats him with casual kindness that he mistakes for encouragement. She'll never see him as more than a helpful employee. This hurts more than he admits.

**Zane Nakamura (Crypto Mule)**
Zane pays Marco $200 per run to move crypto hardware wallets. Marco doesn't understand what he's carrying, doesn't ask, just pockets the cash. It's extra money, guilt-free (or as guilt-free as Marco gets). He has no idea Zane is Val's business partner or that these runs are connected to the trafficking operation.

> **CLUE: Crypto Wallet** — Physical prop: someone brings an old USB drive to the party. This is the "crypto hardware wallet" Marco has been muling. Can be discovered in his valet stand, his go-bag, or planted on the body.

**Irina Volkov (Situationship)**
It started as glances across the hotel lobby. Small nods, conversations that never happened in words. She recognized him as someone trapped — because she is too. He recognized her the same way.

But it became more than recognition. Late nights after shifts, they started talking — really talking. In the kitchen at 2 AM over cold food and warm beer. On the loading dock watching the stars. She told him about Katya, about her mother dying in Moscow, about what Gloria makes her do. He told her about Diego, about Barcelona, about the go-bag under his bed. They are the only two people in each other's lives who know the full truth.

It's not a relationship — neither of them can afford that. It's a situationship built on shared captivity. They sleep together sometimes, hold each other in the dark, and never talk about what happens next because there is no next. He's planning to run to Barcelona. She can't leave without condemning her family. They both know this thing has an expiration date and they're choosing not to read it.

Marco's crush on Val is the performance. Irina is the reality. And leaving her behind might be the thing that keeps him from ever actually getting on that plane.

### Alibi + Versions

**The Alibi**
At valet stand all night. Multiple guests saw him working. Parked three different cars during relevant timeframe. Tip log shows activity throughout evening. Another valet covered for him during routine 15-20 minute break (normal for shift).

**Plausible Version**
Guests confirm seeing Marco. He was consistently visible, charming, professional. Tip log doesn't lie—he was processing transactions. Break timing is routine and covered by coworker. Nothing suspicious about his movements. Why would the valet kill the guest? No obvious motive visible.

### Motive
**EXTREME**

Triple-layered and devastating:

1. **Protecting Mother** — Richie's federal testimony destroys Gloria. Marco loses her, loses the family structure (however toxic), loses the framework his entire life was built around.

2. **Protecting Escape Plan** — If Gloria is arrested, federal attention intensifies. Marco's skimming, fake passport, escape plan all become visible. His future evaporates.

3. **Grief/Revenge for Diego** — His older brother died because Richie provided bad intelligence. Marco has carried that rage for two years. It's not conscious, active rage—it's the kind that lives in your chest and wakes you up at 3 AM. Richie didn't pull the trigger, but Richie made Diego a target. That's murder by intelligence failure.
