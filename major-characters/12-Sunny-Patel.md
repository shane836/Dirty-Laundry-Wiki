## CHARACTER 9: DR. SANJAY "SUNNY" PATEL | The Consultant

**Tagline:** *Federal agent trained to be invisible in plain sight. Failing both the agent part and the human part.*

> **HOST NOTE:** Sunny can be played by any gender — adjust pronouns accordingly throughout all documents.

**Era:** Early forties. Forgettable suit. Aggressive blandness trained to be invisible. Competent in every way except the ones that matter.

**Public Persona:** Least memorable person at conference. Consulting talk—"synergy," "optimization," "leverage," "stakeholder alignment." Business cards from "Stratton Consulting Group." Forgettable suit in forgettable color. Ability to blend into any corporate environment. Background music of the room. Nobody remembers his face five minutes after meeting.

**Real Story:** Federal agent on joint IRS/Interpol task force investigating offshore tax evasion and cartel asset flow. Embedded at conference six months prior. Entire operation hinged on Richie flipping. Cover identity: Stratton Consulting Group (fake McKinsey)—website with fake client testimonials, functional LinkedIn profile with three years of history, WeWork mailing address, the whole apparatus.

Specialties in "enterprise resource optimization" and "cross-border compliance strategy." Cover is so good that Val hired him for hotel legitimacy audit. Over three months, met with Richie six times disguised as business lunches. Richie provided documents, recordings, accounts—enough to take down half the conference attendees. Plan: Richie testifies in three weeks, disappears into witness protection. Career-defining case for Sunny.

But Sunny is uncomfortable. He likes Richie—finds him funny, charming, genuinely remorseful about crimes. Using Richie as bait feels wrong. Pushed superiors to pull Richie out early. They refused, citing compromised assets and timeline pressure. Now Richie is dead and Sunny's career, case, and conscience are in ruins.

**Connection to Richie:** Handler/informant relationship evolved into quasi-friendship. Richie was star witness. Career-defining evidence. Now gone. Also: fed Richie $50K in expense money that Richie spent on luxuries and bribes instead of security. If superiors find out about misspent funds, career over for different reason. Guilt compounds grief.

**Key Relationships:**
- **Richie Montecito**: Handler/informant turned quasi-friend. Genuinely liked him. Feels guilty about death, guilt about money, guilt about exposure.
- **Zane Nakamura**: Zane watching Sunny. Noticed photographing documents during networking sessions. Zane suspects law enforcement. Sunny knows Zane is watching. Cat-and-mouse dynamic.
- **Val Montecito**: Val hired his fake firm for audit. Gives Sunny legitimate hotel access. Makes him feel worse about deception. Knows her brother is dead, can't reveal case status.
- **Gloria Reyes**: Has thick file on Gloria. RICO case building for a year. Intercepted wire transfers, phone records, cartel structure. She has no idea she's under active investigation. Case would devastate her operation.

**Financial Entanglements:**
- Fed Richie $50K misspent on luxuries and bribes
- Gets paid by Val for fake consulting audit (conflict of interest if discovered)
- Investigation budget at risk if operation collapses
- Career jeopardy if misused funds are discovered
- Witness protection budget no longer needed (Richie dead)

**Alibi:**
- **Plausible:** Very visible, very forgettable all night. Multiple witnesses place him everywhere—bar, lobby, networking room, poolside. So boring people remember him around but can't pinpoint when. Professional invisibility works for alibi. Trained agent knows how to build perfect alibi through misdirection.
- **Implausible:** TOO solid. Distributed presence is exactly what trained agent would construct. Has most to lose from Richie's death (investigation blown), so appears least likely killer. But what if Richie threatened to back out? What if Sunny killed to prevent evidence recantation? What if he staged the murder to frame one of his targets? Motive depends on info that surfaces Round 4.

**Motive: PARADOXICAL** (5/10 as traditional killer—9/10 if case dynamics shift)
- Most to lose from Richie's death (investigation devastated)
- Logically least likely killer
- But what if Richie was backing out and threatening to expose the arrangement?
- What if Sunny killed to prevent evidence recantation that would expose his methods?
- What if he staged murder to frame a different target (Gloria, Dex, Val)?
- What if federal pressure forced his hand to eliminate witness liability?
- Problem: every motive analysis flips on unrevealed information

**Red Herrings & Layers:**
- Appears completely uninvested (untrue—entire career depends on this case)
- Forgettability is trained skill, not natural condition
- Feels genuine guilt about Richie (complicates narrative)
- Misspent funds are separate liability from case
- Val hired his firm legitimately (no deception needed)
- Zane's surveillance creates competing law enforcement angle
- Witness would have exposed federal methods and shortcuts if he testified

**Secrets:**
- Undercover IRS/Interpol agent
- Richie was informant providing case-making evidence
- $50K expense money misspent on luxuries/bribes instead of witness security
- Building active RICO case on Gloria
- Pushed to pull Richie out early but was overruled
- Val hired his fake firm (and he's running concurrent investigation on her)
- Zane has identified him as law enforcement
- Career and case both hang on information that's now dead with Richie
